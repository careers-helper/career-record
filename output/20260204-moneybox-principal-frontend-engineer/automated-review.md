## Gaps or Concerns

- **No professional Next.js experience.** While Next.js is listed as a core skill and demonstrated in a personal project, the CV does not show Next.js used in any professional role. For a position where building a Next.js web dashboard is a primary responsibility, we would typically expect candidates to demonstrate Next.js delivery in a production environment. Other strong candidates for this role will likely have shipped Next.js applications professionally.
- **Experience is heavily migration-focused, not greenfield.** Almost every role and bullet point on this CV centres on migration, modernisation, or bringing order to existing systems. Our role is the opposite: building a web platform from scratch. There is no clear evidence of greenfield product delivery on the CV. The candidate may well be capable of it, but the CV does not demonstrate it.
- **Team building at scale is untested.** The CV shows leadership of a 3-person team. We need someone who can hire and grow a team to 6 engineers within a year, which requires significant investment in recruitment, onboarding, and team culture. The jump from 3 to 6 direct reports, combined with greenfield delivery, is a meaningful stretch.
- **No startup experience evident.** The CV shows a Series C fintech (Kova), a large enterprise (Aegon), a health tech company, and government. None of these suggest comfort with the pace, ambiguity, and resource constraints of a scaling startup. The Kova role is closest, but it was a 400-person company, not the earlier-stage environment Moneybox represents.
- **No mention of SEO or web marketing optimisation.** Our nice-to-have around SEO knowledge is not addressed at all. Given that we are expanding our marketing site alongside the dashboard, some SEO awareness would be expected from a principal-level web hire.
- **GraphQL experience appears limited.** Listed under related skills as "GraphQL (Apollo Client)" but no bullet point or role description elaborates on it. For a role where we may adopt GraphQL, we would want to see more substantive evidence.
- **Mobile-first company context is absent.** The CV does not mention any experience working alongside mobile teams or understanding mobile-first product development. Our biggest challenge is extending a mobile-first company to web, and evidence of navigating that specific dynamic would strengthen the application.
- **Community of practice and cross-team influence are strong but management depth is uncertain.** The CV clearly shows influence without authority (45 developers, 8 teams), which is valuable. But the role we are hiring for involves building authority through direct management of a growing team, which is a different skill set.

## Overall Assessment

This is a strong technical candidate with exceptional frontend architecture credentials, particularly in design systems, engineering standards, and cross-team technical leadership. The React and TypeScript depth is exactly what we need, and the regulated industry background is a genuine asset.

The primary concern is fit rather than capability. The CV tells a clear story of someone whose expertise and career arc centres on modernisation, migration, and platform engineering within established organisations. Our role requires greenfield product delivery in a startup context, growing a team from zero to six, and building for a mobile-first company expanding to web. These are meaningfully different challenges from what the CV demonstrates.

The candidate would likely excel at the architectural and standards-setting aspects of this role. The question is whether they would thrive in the greenfield, high-ambiguity, hiring-heavy context. We would want to explore this directly in the interview process, particularly in the system design exercise and leadership conversation.

**Verdict:** Invite to recruiter screen. The technical credentials warrant a conversation, but the greenfield/startup fit and management scaling ambitions should be probed early.
