# Role: Multiple Companies — Early Career Roles (2008 – 2010)

## Snapshot

- **One-liner:** A collection of early technical roles spanning IT support, freelance web design, and contract development work undertaken before and during university.
- **Scope:** Hands-on technical delivery — desktop support, basic web builds, and small-scale application maintenance.
- **Team / org context:** Varied: local government IT department, solo freelance work, and a small commercial web agency.
- **What success looked like:** Gaining foundational professional experience, building basic technical competence, and earning enough to support studies.

---

## Datapoint Solutions — Contract: Junior Developer
**Jun 2009 – Aug 2010**
**Location:** Reading, UK

This was a contract role with a small web agency, initially taken on as a summer placement between second and final year of university and then extended on a part-time basis throughout final year. The work involved maintaining and extending a set of PHP and MySQL web applications for the agency's existing client base. Most of the day-to-day work was bug fixes, minor feature additions, and responding to client change requests.

The most substantial piece of work was building a set of internal reporting dashboards that gave the agency's account managers visibility into project status and client billing. These were straightforward PHP pages pulling data from MySQL, styled with basic CSS. Nothing architecturally notable, but it was the first time the business had any consolidated view of that information.

This was the first role that involved working directly with real end users — both the agency's internal staff and, occasionally, external clients raising support queries. It introduced the reality that software exists to serve people who are not developers, and that their priorities rarely align with what feels technically interesting.

## Bracknell Forest Council — Permanent (Summer Placement): IT Support Technician
**Jul 2008 – Sep 2008**
**Location:** Bracknell, UK

A summer placement in the council's internal IT support team, providing 2nd line desktop support to council staff across multiple offices. The role covered a standard mix of hardware and software troubleshooting, desktop imaging using Symantec Ghost, and user account management in Active Directory.

Most of the work was reactive — responding to tickets logged by the 1st line helpdesk, visiting desks to resolve issues, and occasionally reimaging machines for new starters or role changes. There was no development work involved. The technical ceiling was low, but the exposure to a structured IT environment with change control, ticketing systems, and SLAs was useful grounding.

This was Elena's first professional IT role of any kind. The main takeaway was practical: understanding how IT services operate inside a mid-sized organisation, and learning to communicate technical issues to non-technical staff in a council setting where patience and clarity mattered more than speed.

## Self-Employed — Freelance: Web Designer (Part-Time)
**Jan 2008 – Jun 2009**

Part-time freelance work undertaken alongside a university degree, building basic websites for small local businesses. The typical engagement involved an initial meeting to understand what the client wanted, followed by building a simple brochure-style site using HTML, CSS, and occasionally WordPress where the client needed to update content themselves.

Clients were found through word of mouth and a small amount of local advertising. The work was not technically demanding — most sites were static or used off-the-shelf WordPress themes with minor customisation. Pricing was informal and, in hindsight, well below market rate, but the goal was experience and supplementary income rather than building a sustainable business.

The freelance work overlapped with the start of the Datapoint Solutions contract. Once the contract role became part-time during final year, the freelance work was wound down in favour of the more structured and better-paid agency work.

---

## Positioning notes

These roles represent the earliest professional technical experience in Elena's career, all undertaken before or during her university degree. None of them involved meaningful architectural decision-making, leadership, or significant technical complexity. They are included for completeness and because they established foundational habits: working with real users, operating within professional IT environments, and delivering working software to a deadline — even if the software itself was straightforward. The progression from helpdesk support through freelance web work to a contract development role reflects a natural trajectory toward software engineering, but none of these roles should be interpreted as senior or strategic in any way.
